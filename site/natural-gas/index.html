Railbelt natural gas

Gas from Cook Inlet heats most homes and businesses from Homer to the Mat-Su and fuels about four-fifths of Railbelt electricity. Its price has risen every year since 2023 as the basin's older fields decline, and the next supplies, imported or piped from the North Slope, are likely to cost more.

Prices

What gas costs now

Enstar, the gas utility for Southcentral, buys gas from producers under long-term contracts and passes the cost to customers without markup. That cost is reset each July. On top of it, customers pay Enstar's delivery charges.

Supply

Where the gas comes from

Nearly all of it comes from about 20 fields in and around Cook Inlet, most run by Hilcorp. Production peaked in 1990, when Southcentral also exported LNG and made fertilizer. New wells raised output in 2025, but wells here lose 30 to 40% of their production each year, so drilling has to continue just to hold steady.

Contracts

When today's contracts run out

Utilities buy most of their gas under long-term contracts with Hilcorp. Chugach Electric's ends first, in 2028. The earliest new sources, an import terminal or the North Slope pipeline, are not expected before 2029.

This winter

Will there be enough gas?

Southcentral uses several times more gas on a cold January day than in summer. Producers can't raise output that fast, so utilities fill storage reservoirs in summer and draw them down in winter.
