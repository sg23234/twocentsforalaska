Input costs

Railbelt natural gas

Gas from Cook Inlet heats most homes and businesses from Homer to the Mat-Su and fuels about four-fifths of Railbelt electricity. Its price has risen every year since 2023 as the basin's older fields decline, and the contracts that supply it start running out in 2028.

Prices

What gas costs now

Enstar, the gas utility for Southcentral, buys gas from producers under long-term contracts and passes the cost to customers without markup. That cost is reset each July. On top of it, customers pay Enstar's delivery charges.

Supply

Where the gas comes from

Nearly all of it comes from about 20 fields in and around Cook Inlet, most run by Hilcorp. Production peaked in 1990, when Southcentral also exported LNG and made fertilizer. New wells raised output in 2025, but wells here lose 30 to 40% of their production each year, so drilling has to continue just to hold steady.

Storage

How much gas is in storage

Cook Inlet fields can't raise output fast enough for a cold January, so utilities fill storage in summer and draw it down in winter. Most of the utilities' gas is stored at CINGSA, a depleted gas field near Kenai. Each line below is one storage year, April to March.

Weather

How cold this winter has been

Gas use rises and falls with the temperature. Heating degree days measure how cold it has been: each degree a day's average temperature falls below 65°F adds one. A colder winter means more gas burned and storage drawn down faster.

Notes

  • Mcf is a thousand cubic feet of gas. Bcf is a billion cubic feet, a million Mcf.
  • EIA's city gate price for Alaska is close to what Enstar pays producers, including storage. Customer prices add delivery charges and are averages of all bills in a month, so they swing with the season.
  • The Gas Cost Adjustment is Enstar's own figure for the year ahead, filed each May and in effect from July, subject to review by the Regulatory Commission of Alaska.
  • Contract, production and project details are kept by hand.
