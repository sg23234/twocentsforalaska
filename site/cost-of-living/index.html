Cost of living

The read

Prices in Anchorage and the Mat-Su are up 3.3% from a year ago, close to the national rate, and almost all of the extra pressure is energy. Gasoline is up more than a quarter, while food rose less than 2% and cars, clothing and medical care got cheaper. In rural Alaska, heating fuel has reached a record $7.97 a gallon on average. Over the longer run, prices in urban Alaska have risen less than in the U.S. since 2019, but the state still starts from a high base.

How Alaska compares

Anchorage costs about 28% more than the average U.S. city on a standard basket, and Alaska's residential electricity is about 57% above the U.S. rate. Yet prices have grown more slowly here: from 2019 to 2025, urban Alaska's index rose about seven points less than the national index, mostly because Alaska saw lower inflation in 2020 and 2023.
