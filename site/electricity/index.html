    Power Cost Equalization

    What village households actually pay

    The prices above are what electricity costs to produce and deliver. In about 190 rural communities, the state pays most of a household's bill for its first 750 kWh a month through Power Cost Equalization (PCE). It is one of Alaska's largest rural programs.

    How the credit is set

    1. The base rate. Each year the Regulatory Commission of Alaska averages what households pay per kWh in Anchorage, Fairbanks and Juneau. For the year from July 2026 it is 22.11¢, up from 20.38¢ the year before.
    2. The gap. For each village utility, the gap is its cost per kWh minus the base rate. Costs above $1.00 a kWh don't count.
    3. The state pays 95% of the gap. The credit comes off the bill for a home's first 750 kWh each month. Community buildings such as clinics and water plants get 70 kWh a month per resident. State and federal offices and businesses get nothing.
    4. Past 750 kWh, the household pays the full price.

    About $570 a year for each Alaskan the program reaches: $46 million in FY2025 spread over 81,000 people. That total includes the credit on community buildings, so the amount on home bills is somewhat less.

    The money comes from the earnings of the PCE Endowment Fund, which the Legislature set up in 2000. If earnings fall short, payments are cut in proportion.
