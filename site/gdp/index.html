The read

Alaska's economy shrank in the first half of 2026 after a strong 2025. Real output grew 4.7% in 2025, then fell at a 3.9% annual rate in the first quarter and 1.3% in the second, while the U.S. grew. Even after 2025's gain, inflation-adjusted output is still slightly below its 2012 peak. Oil and gas swing the total, and the state's quarterly figures are often revised by several points.

How Alaska compares

Alaska's economy is the third smallest by output, ahead of only Vermont and Wyoming. Its real GDP has grown far more slowly than the nation's since the late 1990s. Oil prices and production make its quarterly figures swing widely, and in the latest release its first-quarter estimate had the largest revision of any state.

Data notes

  • State GDP comes out about three months after each quarter, together with the third U.S. estimate. Every September, BEA revises several years at once.
  • Real GDP uses chained 2017 dollars. Industry pieces don't add exactly to the total, so the "everything else" row is a rough guide.
  • GDP counts output produced in Alaska. Permanent Fund earnings and dividends are income to Alaskans but are not part of state GDP.
