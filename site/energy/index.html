The read

Electricity in Alaska costs about 57% more than the national average, and the state's main source of heat and power is running short. Southcentral Alaska gets more than 70% of its electricity from Cook Inlet natural gas, and the region's gas utility says it could fall about 18 days short of supply this winter if deliveries stay at their current pace. Imported gas is expected to cost more: the Asian spot price is more than double what the utility pays now. In rural Alaska, state support brings the first block of household power closer to urban rates, while heating fuel hit a record $7.97 a gallon.

How Alaska compares

Alaska's average residential electricity price is about 57% above the U.S. rate and sixth-highest among states, and rural communities on diesel generation pay several times the urban rate before state support. The Railbelt has no pipeline connection to other gas basins and depends almost entirely on Cook Inlet, so falling production there affects prices across the region.

Data notes

  • The Alaska average electricity price blends Railbelt and rural utilities. Most Railbelt households pay less than the average; many rural households pay far more before PCE.
  • Gas prices here are utility costs, not customer bills. Enstar adjusts rates each summer and says bills are likely to rise in coming winters.
