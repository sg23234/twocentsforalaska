ANC air cargo

Partly live · draft

Anchorage sits on the great-circle route between Asia and North America, within about nine and a half hours' flying of 90% of the industrialized world, and most trans-Pacific freighters stop there to refuel. That makes Ted Stevens Anchorage International the busiest cargo airport in the United States and the third busiest in the world, though most of the freight is only passing through.

Cargo handled each year

Freight and mail loaded, unloaded or passing through ANC, in millions of metric tons. Anchorage counts transit freight that never leaves the plane during a refueling stop, which most airports don't.

How ANC compares

Two ways to rank cargo airports: tons of freight and mail handled (ACI, worldwide) and the landed weight of cargo planes (FAA, U.S. only).

The flights

Cargo planes now make up most landings at ANC. The big international freighters are mostly Boeing 747s and 777s; a fleet of smaller planes carries freight on to the rest of Alaska.

Jet fuel

Refueling is the main reason freighters stop in Anchorage, so jet fuel sold is the most direct gauge of that business. Statewide sales are dominated by ANC; the state now reports ANC's own figure for recent fiscal years.

What passes through

In 2021, the latest year studied in detail, 1.7 million metric tons stayed aboard during refueling stops and 2.0 million were loaded or unloaded. About 63% of Asia-to-U.S. air cargo went through Anchorage, and 47% of U.S.-to-Asia.

Trade that clears customs in Anchorage

Goods that clear customs at the Anchorage port of entry are a slice of what flies through. In 2023 air imports recorded there were worth $34.7 billion and air exports $18.5 billion. By weight the leading goods were electrical equipment and machinery, then instruments, plastics and toys; fish and seafood ranked eighth.

Jobs

Payroll jobs at airlines in Alaska, live from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. This counts passenger and cargo airlines alike; ground handling, fueling and freight forwarding are counted in other industries.

What's changing

Trade rules

The U.S. ended duty-free entry for low-value parcels (the $800 de minimis exemption) on goods from China in May 2025 and on goods from everywhere that August. Cross-border e-commerce by air fell sharply, and NorthLink Aviation's planned e-commerce hub at ANC stalled. Anchorage's tonnage kept growing anyway as general freight and electronics filled the space. FedEx cut its Anchorage landings by about 12% in the first nine months of 2025, while Kalitta Air, China Airlines and EVA Air added flights.

Jet fuel

Freighters stop in Anchorage mainly to refuel, and more than 70% of the airport's jet fuel arrives by tanker from Asian refineries. In March 2026, with the Strait of Hormuz disrupted and China limiting refined fuel exports, international cargo landings fell 8% from a year earlier, the first real monthly decline in over a year. By August they were 5% above a year earlier.

Room to park

Heavy snowstorms have shown how tight wide-body parking is at ANC: carriers with reserved spots keep landing, others divert. Private projects on airport land aim to add capacity: NorthLink Aviation's 120-acre South Airpark campus (a 55-year lease, with up to 11 drive-through hardstands planned), a cold-storage warehouse and hardstands by Alaska Cold Cargo Storage on the North Airpark, and six hardstands proposed by Alaska Airport Partners on the West Airpark. The state also lists $126 million for nine more airport-run hardstands.

Fairbanks as the backup

Fairbanks International, about 260 miles north, is ANC's main diversion airport. Its cargo tonnage nearly tripled in the first half of 2026, from 12,161 to 35,399 metric tons, as carriers used it during storms and summer construction at ANC.

Data notes

  • Tonnage counts freight and mail loaded, unloaded or in transit. Anchorage is the only top-10 airport whose count includes transit freight that stays aboard; in 2021 that was about 46% of its total, so ANC's world rank overstates how much freight is handled on the ground.
  • Landed weight is the certificated maximum landing weight of the all-cargo aircraft that land, summed over the year. It measures flights, not freight, and is what the FAA uses to share out cargo grants to airports.
  • ACI revises earlier years; the series here uses each year's figure as published with that year's ranking. The 2014 figure is from Air Cargo World. 2025 is preliminary.
  • The 2021 economic impact study gives two figures for Anchorage's share of Asia-to-U.S. air cargo: 51% in its summary and 63% in its tables. The page uses the table figure.
  • Recent-month landings are NorthLink Aviation's counts of international cargo landings, compiled from airport data and reported in the trade press. NorthLink is developing cargo facilities at ANC.
