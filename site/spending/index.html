The read

The state's general-purpose budget for FY2027 is $6.6 billion, about the same as FY2026, and 60% of the money to pay for it comes from the Permanent Fund draw. Federal dollars flowing into Alaska are larger still: at about $24,800 per resident, Alaska receives more federal obligations per person than any other state. Government accounts for about 18% of Alaska's real output, and government payroll jobs fell by 1,900 over the past year while private payrolls grew.

How Alaska compares

Alaska has no statewide sales or personal income tax, so most of its general-purpose budget is paid for by Permanent Fund earnings and oil revenue rather than by residents. It also receives the most federal obligations per resident of any state. Military bases, federal land agencies and tribal health programs are among the large recipients.

Data notes

  • There is no single official split of Alaska spending into federal, state and private. This page shows each piece from its own source and compares each with GDP, rather than adding them, because federal grants pass through the state budget and would be counted twice.
  • Federal obligations are commitments to spend, including contracts, grants, direct payments and loans, by place of performance. The per-resident figure is from USAspending data as reported by 24/7 Wall St. in Feb 2026; a live version would pull USAspending directly by fiscal year and award type.
  • Private spending is the hardest piece to measure. A future version will add private wages and investment from BEA, and personal income by source, including federal transfers and dividends.
  • The FY2027 summary shows two figures for agency operations ($4,970.8M current-year and $4,988.9M including supplementals); this page uses the second.
