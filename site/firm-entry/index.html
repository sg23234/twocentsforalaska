Firm entry rate

Live data

The share of Alaska's employer businesses that are brand new: firms in their first year with paid employees, as a percentage of all employer firms. A steady flow of new firms is one of the main ways an economy finds new products, raises productivity and creates jobs. The measure comes from the Census Bureau's Business Dynamics Statistics, which covers every employer in the country back to 1978 but is published with a lag of about two years.

The long view

In 1978 about one U.S. employer firm in seven was new. In Alaska, in the pipeline and early oil-revenue years, it was one in five. Alaska's rate fell to about the national rate after the 1986 oil-price crash, slipped below it around 2000, and has been roughly a point below since. Both have drifted down over the whole period.

New firms and the jobs they bring

The count of new firms moves with the size of the economy; the share of jobs at those firms shows how much hiring they account for in their first year.

By industry

Firm age isn't published by industry for individual states, so this compares the establishment entry rate: new business locations as a share of all locations. Small Alaska industries bounce around from year to year, so the comparison uses the 2019–2023 average.

A quicker read: openings and closings

Business Dynamics Statistics run about two years behind. The BLS Business Employment Dynamics series counts jobs at establishments that opened or closed each quarter, through Q4 2024.

Related measures

Three ways to count new businesses

Business applications (Census Business Formation Statistics) count filings for an employer ID number. They are monthly and current, but many applications never become businesses with employees.

The firm entry rate on this page counts businesses that actually started paying employees. It is the most complete measure, covering every employer, but it is annual and about two years old.

Business Employment Dynamics (BLS) counts jobs at establishments that opened or closed, quarterly, with a lag of about seven months. It counts locations, not firms.

Data notes

  • A firm is a business and all the locations it owns. A firm is age 0 in the year it first reports paid employees, measured in March. Firms with no employees (sole proprietors, most gig work) are not counted.
  • Entry rate = age-0 firms ÷ all employer firms in the same year, the standard Census startup-rate definition. The U.S. figures use the national table; Census notes that firm counts summed across states can run slightly higher than the national total, because a firm operating in several states is counted once in each.
  • Figures are from the 2023 vintage of the Business Dynamics Statistics. Each new vintage can revise earlier years.
  • The data refresh reads the Census Bureau's published CSV tables, checks for a new vintage on every run, and needs no API key.
  • Alaska has about 15,000 employer firms, so 150 more or fewer new firms moves the rate by a full percentage point. Read single-year changes with care.
