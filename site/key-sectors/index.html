The read

Alaska added 300 jobs over the year to September 2026, with private gains in transportation, construction, oil and gas and mining offsetting 1,900 fewer government jobs. Oil prices and North Slope output are both up, mine output hit a record $6.5 billion in 2025, and Anchorage handled more all-cargo landed weight than any other U.S. airport. Summer visitor numbers were flat in 2025 after several years of growth, and Bristol Bay's 2026 salmon run was below average but worth more than usual.

How Alaska compares

Alaska's largest employers by industry are trade and transportation, health care, local government and leisure and hospitality, much like most states. What sets it apart is a set of small-workforce, high-output industries: oil and gas employs under 3% of payroll workers but drives much of state revenue and GDP, and Anchorage moves more all-cargo weight than any other U.S. airport.

Data notes

  • Job figures are a survey of employers and exclude the self-employed, which matters for commercial fishing: most fishermen are not in payroll counts.
  • Uniformed military personnel are not in payroll jobs. A military panel with active-duty counts and defense spending is planned.
  • Mining and Bristol Bay values are preliminary and in current dollars. Statewide 2026 salmon totals come later in the year.
